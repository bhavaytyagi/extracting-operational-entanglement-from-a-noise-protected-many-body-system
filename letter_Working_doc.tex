{\it Introduction}---We traditionally treat noise as the enemy of coherence, and typical solutions include isolating
the system better, or correct its errors~\cite{Acharya2023}. Most treatments overlook a third solution: what can
the environment actually \emph{see}? An environment destroys only what it can resolve. A bath connected to $L$ qubits through $L$
independent noise channels can distinguish the coupled spin component at each
site separately. However, a bath connected through one shared mode
sees only a weighted sum of the coupled spin components, or their total for equal couplings~\cite{Blais2021}. Spatial correlation makes an environment partially blind. Spatially correlated noise can dominate the dephasing
of trapped-ion registers~\cite{Kielpinski2001,Langer2005,Monz2011,Dasu2025},
and experiments measure it directly for superconducting
qubits sharing an engineered source~\cite{vonLupke2020} and for silicon spin
qubits~\cite{Yoneda2023}, and in principle from single-qubit operations
alone~\cite{Gulacsi2025}. Decoherence-free subspaces and noiseless subsystems
store information in states a collective bath cannot
distinguish~\cite{Zanardi1997,Lidar1998,Knill2000}, and
experiments confirm them in ions~\cite{Kielpinski2001,Langer2005},
photons~\cite{Kwiat2000} and NMR~\cite{Viola2001}. In these scenarios, one typically prepares the states in advance, and engineers the dynamics to keep them protected. Our earlier work explored a different route:
collective behavior such as synchronization induced by a shared correlated
environment for two coupled spins~\cite{Tyagi2024} and for oscillators
sampled from many initial states~\cite{Bittner2025jcp}, with implications for protecting quantum
resources and coherent transport.

\emph{In this Letter} we extend our work to a many-body system and ask what a
correlated bath does to the entanglement generated by the internal dynamics
of a driven Ising spin chain, a model of many-body quantum chaos.
For $0\le\xi\le1$, ensuring a completely positive (CP) evolution, the bath-correlation parameter $\xi$ is the fraction of
each qubit's noise power shared with every other and sets how much the bath
can see. Here $L$ is the number of spins, or the system volume. At $\xi=0$ the $L$ spatial noise channels are independent; at $\xi=1$
only their common mode remains [Eq.~\eqref{eq:noise_endpoints}].
For dephasing at finite $L\ge3$, nonzero noise and the nontrivial kick
specified below, every physical $\xi<1$ eventually gives zero negativity.
Near $\xi=1$, residual independent noise sets a slow time scale and, when
the limiting slow evolution has a distinct last crossing of the chosen
entanglement threshold at positive finite slow time, with nonzero slope
and no later return, the lifetime $\tau\equiv t_d$ grows as
$(1-\xi)^{-1}$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:lifetime}]. At $\xi=1$, initial states
supported on vectors unchanged by every rotation and reflection of the
ring approach a symmetry-determined floor [Eq.~\eqref{eq:fixed_point}].
We establish the required absence of further invariant subspaces within
this sector for $3\le L\le16$ at the stated self-dual point; larger sizes
remain conditional on that property. We also analyze zero- and
finite-temperature amplitude damping: its stationary state is not fixed
by symmetry alone, and in some cases negativity survives even for $\xi<1$.

{\it Model}---The system is the self-dual kicked Ising ring, a minimal locally
interacting model of many-body quantum chaos~\cite{Bertini2018,Bertini2019},
\begin{align}
\label{eq:SysHam}
\hf[\bm h;t] &= \HI[\bm h]+\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}\delta(t-m)\,\HK ,\\
\HI &= J\sum_{j=1}^{L}\sigma^z_j\sigma^z_{j+1}+\sum_{j=1}^{L}h_j\sigma^z_j ,
\qquad
\HK = b\sum_{j=1}^{L}\sigma^x_j , \nonumber
\end{align}
with periodic boundary conditions, so the one-period Floquet operator is
$U_F[\bm h]=e^{-i\HK}e^{-i\HI[\bm h]}\equiv\UK\UI$. We work at the self-dual
point $J=\pi/4$, $b=-\pi/4$, where the circuit is
dual-unitary~\cite{BertiniClaeysProsen2026}. The cited spectral-form-factor
results have disorder-averaging and time-sector qualifications; they do not
prove chaos for every finite uniform-field ring~\cite{Bertini2018}.
The fixed-point argument below does not require chaotic spectral statistics.
Numerics for the selected map use $h_j=h=1$ and
$\ket{+}^{\otimes L}$. Resonant choices of $J$ or $b$ can change the
fixed algebra, so finite parameter scans do not establish universality.

The ring couples to a bath whose local noise channels can be correlated,
through a Gorini--Kossakowski--Sudarshan--Lindblad
generator~\cite{Lindblad1976,Gorini1976,Breuer2007} with site-basis dissipator
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dissipator_site}
\mathcal L^{(\xi)}_{\rm diss}[\rho]=\sum_a\gamma_a\!\!\sum_{i,j=1}^{L}\!\!
\Xi_{ij}(\xi)\Bigl[\ell^{(a)}_i\rho\,\ell^{(a)\dagger}_j
-\tfrac12\bigl\{\ell^{(a)\dagger}_j\ell^{(a)}_i,\rho\bigr\}\Bigr],
\end{equation}
where $a$ labels the microscopic process: $\ell^{(z)}_j=\sigma^z_j$ for
dephasing, the channel we lead with, and
$\ell^{(-)}_j=\sigma^-_j$ (or both $\sigma^\mp_j$ with rates
$\gamma_\mp=\gamma(n_{\rm th}+\tfrac12\pm\tfrac12)$ at finite temperature) for
amplitude damping. Channels are considered separately. To match the
computational convention, $\sigma^-=c=\ket0\!\bra1$,
$\sigma^+=c^\dagger$, and $\sigma^z=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$;
thus $[J^z,\sum_j c_j]=+\sum_jc_j$. The superscript on $c$ is a channel
label, not a lowering operation relative to this $J^z$.
The noise-correlation matrix is
$\bm\Xi(\xi)=\mathbf I+\xi\mathbf A$, with $\mathbf A$ the adjacency matrix of
a \emph{bath-correlation graph} chosen independently of the ring the
Hamiltonian lives on (Fig.~\ref{fig:model}). Since $\bm\Xi$ is a polynomial in
$\mathbf A$ it shares its eigenvectors $v_\alpha$ and has eigenvalues
$\lambda^\Xi_\alpha=1+\xi\lambda_\alpha$, so
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dissipator_site} becomes a sum of independent collective channels
with jump operators $\ell^{(a)}_\alpha=\sum_jv_\alpha(j)\ell^{(a)}_j$ and
weights $\gamma_a\lambda^\Xi_\alpha\ge0$~\cite{SM}; a channel goes \emph{dark}
when its weight vanishes. We use the complete bath-correlation graph $K_L$:
every pair of distinct sites has the same correlation,
$\Xi_{ij}=(1-\xi)\delta_{ij}+\xi$.
Writing $\mathcal D[B]\rho=B\rho B^\dagger-\{B^\dagger B,\rho\}/2$,
substitution into Eq.~\eqref{eq:dissipator_site} gives the endpoints directly:
\begin{align}
\mathcal L^{(0)}_{\rm diss}&=\sum_a\gamma_a\sum_j\mathcal D[\ell_j^{(a)}],\nonumber\\
\mathcal L^{(1)}_{\rm diss}&=\sum_a\gamma_a\mathcal D\!\left[\sum_j\ell_j^{(a)}\right].
\label{eq:noise_endpoints}
\end{align}
The first has independent local jumps, the second one collective jump per
microscopic process $a$. Thus thermal emission and absorption share one
spatial mode but retain two jump operators.
For $K_L$, $\mathbf A=\mathbf J-\mathbf I$, with $\mathbf J$ the all-ones matrix, has two
eigenvalues, $\lambda_0=L-1$ for the uniform mode and $\lambda_\perp=-1$ with
multiplicity $L-1$, so that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:complete_graph_split}
\mathcal L^{(\xi)}_{\rm diss}=\bigl[1+(L-1)\xi\bigr]\mathcal D_0
+(1-\xi)\,\mathcal D_\perp ,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal D_0$ sums the dissipators of the uniform jumps
$\ell^{(a)}_0=L^{-1/2}\sum_j\ell^{(a)}_j$ and $\mathcal D_\perp$ sums the
$L-1$ relative modes, both including the rates $\gamma_a$.
The matrix $\bm\Xi(\xi)$ is positive semidefinite precisely on
$-1/(L-1)\le\xi\le1$. At $\xi=1$ all $L-1$ relative channels vanish at once,
$\operatorname{rank}\bm\Xi$ collapses from $L$ to $1$, and each surviving
jump is collective: proportional to $J^z=\tfrac12\sum_j\sigma_j^z$ for
dephasing, or to $C=\sum_jc_j$ and, at nonzero temperature, $C^\dagger$
for amplitude damping. Collective dephasing therefore resolves total
longitudinal spin, but cannot distinguish configurations with the same
total longitudinal spin. The bath remains coupled to the ring.

For dephasing, the dissipative step is the exact average over classical
Gaussian white phase noise with covariance $\gamma\bm\Xi$, with no
weak-coupling expansion in $\gamma$ or $L$ (Appendix). Here $\Xi_{jk}$ is
the correlation of equal-time increments, or the normalized white spectrum;
pointwise white-noise variance is not finite. A single shared line realizes
$\xi=1$, while the common-plus-independent power-fraction realization applies
to $0\le\xi\le1$. Amplitude damping instead uses a quantum-bath model.
Its microscopic applicability needs bath correlation times and secular
conditions, including collective transition rates that can scale as
$\gamma L^2$; $L\gamma$ alone is not a sufficient small parameter~\cite{SM}.

We follow the entanglement across a bipartition $A|A^c$, $A=\{1,\dots,N_A\}$,
with the logarithmic negativity
$\EN(\rho)=\log\lVert\rho^{T_A}\rVert_1=\log(1+2\mathcal N)$, $\mathcal N$
being the sum of the absolute values of the negative eigenvalues of
$\rho^{T_A}$~\cite{Peres1996,Horodecki1996,Vidal2002,Plenio2005}. For a
globally pure state this equals the R\'enyi-$1/2$ entropy of
$\rho_A$~\cite{SM}, and in the closed system it gives the class-$\mathcal T$
baseline $\EN(t)=2t\log2$ in its pre-wrap regime
$2t\le\min(N_A,N_B)$~\cite{SM}. We use period one and explicitly define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:selected_map}
\mathcal M_\xi(\rho)=e^{\mathcal L^{(\xi)}_{\rm diss}}
 [\UK\UI\rho\UI^\dagger\UK^\dagger].
\end{equation}
The bath acts after both coherent layers. The dynamical figures use this
selected map. Retained historical fit tables and additional scans in the
supplement instead use $\UI\UK$ followed by noise; their provenance is stated
locally and their data have not been relabeled.

{\it The lifetime diverges}---For dephasing, $L\ge3$, $\gamma>0$,
$\sin(2b)\ne0$ and every physical $\xi<1$, the unique attractor is
$\openone/2^L$. This includes the singular boundary
$\xi=-1/(L-1)$~\cite{SM}. The reason is that an undamped operator must
commute with the dephasing jumps and their kick conjugates. These generate
the local Pauli algebra, leaving only the identity; at the negative
boundary the jumps are differences of local spins, which give the same
conclusion for $L\ge3$ (SM, Sec.~S5A). Thus all other modes decay.
The maximally mixed state's partial transpose is strictly positive,
so every trajectory has positive partial transpose (PPT) at all sufficiently
late periods. Zero negativity
means PPT, not necessarily separability. It does not imply a linear approach
to zero or prohibit earlier revivals. In zero-temperature amplitude damping,
$L=4$, $\gamma=.01$, the unique stationary state has a negative partial
transpose (NPT) at $\xi=.9$ and $.99$: its smallest PT eigenvalues are
$-.008422733738$ and $-.016204546990$, respectively (SM, Sec.~S5A).
Thus amplitude damping can retain entanglement even with independent noise
present; it has no universal sudden-death result.

The factor $1-\xi$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:complete_graph_split} is the strength
of the noise that restores site resolution. It produces slow leakage away
from the collective stationary states. For dephasing let $Q$ project an
operator onto the full space of endpoint stationary operators, and let
$d_{\rm loc}=\sum_j\mathcal D[\sigma_j^z]$. Write
$s=\gamma(1-\xi)n$. On the primary input the leading slow density is
$R(s)=e^{sQd_{\rm loc}Q}Q\rho_+$, with
$\rho_+=(\ket{+}\!\bra{+})^{\otimes L}$.
Here the other decaying modes must relax on a time scale that stays finite
as $\xi\to1$ at fixed $L$ and $\gamma>0$.
Define $s_d$ as the last downward crossing of a specified positive
negativity threshold by $R(s)$. An \emph{isolated transverse final crossing}
means that this crossing is distinct, has nonzero downward slope, and is
followed by no return above the threshold. For exact zero negativity,
transversality instead means that the last negative eigenvalues of
$R(s)^{T_A}$ pass through zero into the positive region; its derivative
must be positive on the entire zero-eigenvalue subspace at the crossing.
It does not mean that negativity becomes negative. Since the crossing then
occurs at a finite slow time $s_d$, the corresponding lifetime is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lifetime}
t_d(\xi)=\frac{A}{1-\xi}+O(1),\qquad \gamma A=s_d.
\end{equation}
Integer-period sampling changes only the bounded correction. The Appendix
states the threshold convention; SM Secs.~S5B--S5C give the slow-state
derivation and full crossing conditions.
Amplitude damping does not preserve the maximally mixed state (it is
\emph{nonunital}), so its stationary states and slow generator must be
computed separately. We do this in SM Sec.~S5D, retaining the evolution
within the dissipative step. The same inverse law applies when that slow
state satisfies the crossing conditions; the four-spin counterexample
above shows why this is not guaranteed.
A relaxation gap alone does not determine $A$.

Figure~\ref{fig:lifetime} uses newly generated selected-map trajectories with
frozen source and parameter manifests. Every source is truncated at $t\le150$
before selecting training crossings, the onset or the fitted coefficient.
The coefficients are $A=53.784$, $26.423$, $59.090$ in legend order;
later samples enter only the observed crossings and held-out comparisons.
Each lifetime record retains at least $150$ periods and ends after $100$
successive samples with minimum PT eigenvalue above $10^{-10}$ following an

{\it The floor, in closed form}---Let $\mathcal S$ contain the vectors
unchanged by every rotation and reflection of the ring. The coherent
layers with uniform fields and the collective dephasing jump preserve
$\mathcal S$. An initial density matrix supported there is a mixture of
such vectors, including coherent superpositions within this sector; this
is stronger than requiring its density matrix to be invariant under the
ring symmetries.
The surviving jump is $L_z=2\sqrt\gamma J^z$. For $\gamma>0$ and
$\sin(2b)\ne0$, the endpoint fixed algebra is
$\mathfrak A=\{J^x,J^y,J^z,\UI\}'$, where the prime denotes all operators
commuting with every listed operator and
$J^\alpha=\tfrac12\sum_j\sigma_j^\alpha$. It is independent of $\gamma$,
uniform $h$, and nonresonant $b$; its dependence on $J$ requires a separate
check. It can exceed the represented dihedral group algebra. If its
restriction to $\mathcal S$ contains only scalar multiples of the identity,
there is no further invariant subspace under these operators within
$\mathcal S$. This is the required \emph{irreducibility}.
Dephasing preserves the identity, so the unique stationary density in this
sector is its normalized projector. The Appendix also excludes persistent
oscillations in this sector, establishing convergence of every supported
initial state to

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fixed_point}
\rho_\infty=\frac{\Psym}{N_L} ,
\qquad
\ENinf=\log\bigl\lVert(\Psym/N_L)^{T_A}\bigr\rVert_1 ,
\end{equation}
where $\Psym=\lvert G\rvert^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}U(g)$ projects onto the states
invariant under the full ring symmetry group $G=D_L$ ($L$ rotations, $L$
reflections) and $N_L=\Tr\Psym$ is the number of binary \emph{bracelets} of
length $L$: $N_L=6,13,30,78,224$ for $L=4,6,8,10,12$. The derivation, and the
restricted-commutant condition it rests on, are in the Appendix.
We check irreducibility by generating the sector from the fully polarized
vector with the collective-spin and Ising operators: the resulting span
has dimension $N_L$, and the polarized rank-one projector belongs to their
algebra. Together these facts force every commuting operator to be scalar.
Exact integer arithmetic modulo a prime certifies full rank for every
$3\le L\le16$ at $J=\pi/4$ (Appendix; rank tables and implementation checks
in SM Sec.~S9). Larger sizes require the same irreducibility property,
which has not been proved for arbitrary $L$. At $L=10$ the balanced-cut
value is $0.823820166737$. Generic symmetry-breaking fields can destroy the
protected sector, but not every nonuniform field changes the channel:
shifting a single $h_j$ by $\pi$ gives only a scalar phase.

Equation~\eqref{eq:fixed_point} is a statement about collective
\emph{dephasing}. The selected-map amplitude plateaus at $L=10$ are
$.838397020906$ for zero temperature, $\gamma=.01$, and $.825852260622$
for $n_{\rm th}=1$, $\gamma=.003$. Their parameter dependence is real:
the amplitude stationary state is not fixed by the dephasing projector.

The state $\rho_\infty$ is an equal mixture of normalized orbit superpositions.
Each orbit has at most $2L$ basis vectors. Convexity of the PT trace norm and
the pure-state Schmidt-rank bound therefore give
$\ENinf\le\min[\log(2L),\min(N_A,N_B)\log2]$.
The floor is nonextensive; the bound does not establish an asymptotic
logarithmic coefficient or a cut-independent area law. At $L=10$ its
negativity in bits is $1.19$, which is not a count of distillable Bell pairs.
The value depends on the cut. A six-site reduction respecting the original
balanced cut has negative partial transpose~\cite{SM}, so a whole-ring
measurement is not necessary for all entanglement detection.

{\it Symmetry, not scrambling}---The collective bath contributes $J^z$
to the interaction/noise algebra; $J^z$ itself is not conserved by the kick.
For uniform fields the fixed algebra is unchanged at $h=0$, so the floor is
not a diagnostic of chaos. Legacy scans of nonuniform fields and lower-degree
bath graphs are retained in the supplement with their parameter ranges.
They do not establish a universal graph-necessity theorem or show that every
symmetry-breaking perturbation removes the floor.

Every dihedral isotypic projector $\Pi_\lambda$ commutes with the coherent
layers and collective jump, so $w_\lambda=\Tr(\Pi_\lambda\rho)$ is conserved.
Under the scalar restricted-commutant condition, the basin of the state in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fixed_point} is exactly $w_{A_1}=1$, equivalently
$\Psym\rho\Psym=\rho$. Density-matrix group invariance alone is weaker:
$\openone/2^L$ is group invariant but has $w_{A_1}=N_L/2^L$.
The plus, fully polarized, GHZ and $W$ states are in the supported basin.
The N\'eel input has $w_{A_1}=1/2$ for even $L\ge4$ and needs its separate
sector limit~\cite{SM}. Outside this basin nonfixed peripheral modes can
also prevent ordinary convergence; at $L=6$ period-two states exist.

{\it Experimental reach}---For the finite-size dephasing slow limit,
$\gamma A$ is independent of fixed positive $\gamma$. With
$T_2=1/(2\gamma)$ and algebraic slow crossing $s_*$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tdT2}
\frac{t_d}{T_2}=\frac{2s_*}{1-\xi}+O(\gamma).
\end{equation}
At $L=10$, $2s_*=.557070700112$; at $L=6,8$ it is
$.417656007048$ and $.513783167248$, respectively.
For the $10^{-3}$ threshold the $L=10$ coefficient is $.529792058624$.
This dimensionless relation is conditional on the white-noise protocol,
initial state, size, cut and final-crossing convention; it is not a universal
device lifetime or a derived large-size limit.

Two-qubit noise measurements motivate an experimental test but do not by
themselves calibrate the equicorrelated white-noise matrix of a ten-qubit
ring. For an ideal two-qubit white-dephasing model,
$1-\xi=\Gamma_{\rm DFS}/(2\Gamma_{\rm test})$, with
$\Gamma_{\rm test}=2\gamma$ the single-qubit coherence rate and
$\Gamma_{\rm DFS}$ the decay rate of $\ket{01}\!\bra{10}$; applying that inference
requires the same source spectrum and normalization in both measurements.
Experiments with deliberately shared noise~\cite{Kielpinski2001} and measured
spin-qubit correlations~\cite{Yoneda2023} do not alone justify inserting
their values into Eq.~\eqref{eq:tdT2}. A global field or beam supplies a
possible shared line, and trapped-ion kicked-Ising dynamics offers a setting
to test the protocol~\cite{Kyprianidis2021}.

Uniformity is a separate constraint. With static longitudinal disorder
$h_j=1+w\eta_j$, $\eta_j\in[-1,1]$, define
$K=-i[\sum_j\eta_j\sigma_j^z,\,\cdot\,]$ and
$\overline R=(I-\mathcal M_1)^{-1}(I-Q)$, taking the inverse only off the
full $+1$ space. The leading generator is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:budget}
G_2=\tfrac12QK^2Q+QK\overline R\mathcal M_1KQ.
\end{equation}
It depends on $\gamma$ through the endpoint map and reduced inverse.
At fixed finite $L$ and positive $\gamma$, for the primary input with no
nonfixed peripheral component, a separated stable spectrum and a regular
final crossing give $t_d=s_*(\gamma,\eta)/w^2+O(1/w)$.
Disorder in a shared-line coupling has a different leading generator,
$\gamma Q\mathcal D[\sum_j\eta_j\sigma_j^z]Q$.
Even when effective generators add, their inverse threshold times need not.
The old numerical inverse-time budget mixed sizes, thresholds and width
conventions and is not a calibrated constraint on a device. A combined-noise
calculation with matched conventions is still required~\cite{SM}.

At $L=10$, a six-site entanglement witness can use
$A=\{4,5\}$ and $B=\{6,8,9,10\}$ (one-based sites) within the original
balanced cut; its reduced state's minimum PT eigenvalue is
$-.0120172454023$~\cite{SM}. Quantifying the full-ring negativity remains a
different measurement. The $2L$ permutation expectations are useful symmetry
diagnostics but are not entanglement witnesses: both the floor state and the
separable $\ket{0\cdots0}$ give unity. Their decay alone does not calibrate
an entanglement lifetime or a universal shot budget.

{\it Discussion}---Classical correlated dephasing preserves separability
of separable inputs in the absence of an entangling gate. With the Ising
layer, the dynamics generates entanglement and the collective symmetry
constrains its surviving state. Collective amplitude damping can itself
create entanglement and follows a separate nonunital mechanism.
A nonzero dephasing floor represents persistent mixed-state entanglement, not demonstrated
storage of an arbitrary unknown qubit: the supported basin has a single
attracting density matrix.

The lifetime can probe residual independent noise when the protocol and
crossing are calibrated. Our conclusions are Markovian, finite-size or
explicitly conditional; the exact commutant certificate covers $3\le L\le16$,
and no thermodynamic lifetime-prefactor theorem is established. The regenerated
dynamical figures test the selected map on the declared finite $L=10$ grid;
the additional historical scans retain their separate legacy provenance.

\section*{Appendix}

\paragraph*{Derivation of the fixed point.}
The following steps distinguish a fixed algebra, its peripheral extension,
and the basin needed for the floor.

\emph{1. Endpoint algebra.} For $\gamma>0$, the dephasing exponential is a
Hilbert--Schmidt contraction with equality space $\{J^z\}'$.
Consequently $\mathcal M_1X=\lambda X$, $|\lambda|=1$, is equivalent to
$[J^z,X]=0$ and $U_FXU_F^\dagger=\lambda X$.

\emph{1.1.} Conjugating the first condition with $U_F$ gives commutation
with $\cos(2b)J^z-\sin(2b)J^y$. If $\sin(2b)\ne0$, commutation with
$J^y$ and $J^x$ follows. Hence
\begin{align}
\label{eq:em_kraus_algebra}
\mathfrak A&={\rm Fix}(\mathcal M_1)=\{J^x,J^y,J^z,\UI\}',\\
{\rm Per}_\lambda(\mathcal M_1)
 &=\{X:[X,J^{x,y,z}]=0,\ \UI X\UI^\dagger=\lambda X\}.\nonumber
\end{align}
The projection $Q$ onto $\mathfrak A$ is the trace-preserving conditional
expectation~\cite{Kribs2003,Arias2002}; it gives the Ces\`aro limit for all
states. The full peripheral space need not equal $\mathfrak A$.

\emph{1.2.} Each peripheral operator is scalar on the unique maximal-spin
carrier. Evaluating its $\UI$ eigenoperator equation on the fully polarized
state forces that scalar to vanish if $\lambda\ne1$. Thus all nonfixed
peripheral operators annihilate that carrier, including the plus input.
Its ordinary limit is $Q\rho_+$.

\emph{2. Supported basin.} Let $\mathcal S$ be the vectors fixed by all
$U(g)$, $g\in D_L$. Its projector is $\Psym$ and its dimension is $N_L$.
The condition is $\Psym\rho_0\Psym=\rho_0$, equivalently
$\Tr[U(g)\rho_0]=1$ for every $g$. Neither density-matrix invariance nor
$|\Tr[U(g)\rho_0]|=1$ is the required condition.

\emph{2.1.} If the restricted commutant on $\mathcal S$ is scalar,
its only stationary density is $\Psym/N_L$. A nonzero nonfixed peripheral operator
there would have $X^\dagger X$ scalar and so be invertible on $\mathcal S$,
contradicting step 1.2 on the polarized vector. Ordinary convergence follows
for every supported density, without an additional trace criterion.

\emph{3. Finite-size certificate.} At $J=\pi/4$ the ring's $ZZ$ layer is,
up to scalar phase, $R=(-1)^{n_{\rm DW}/2}$. In the unnormalized bracelet
basis, $\sum_j\sigma_j^x$ and joint weight/$R$ projectors have integer
entries. Their orbit from the all-zero vector has full rank modulo prime
$1000003$ for every $3\le L\le16$; a second prime $1000033$ checks the
even sizes in this range and $L=13,15$~\cite{SM}. Full modular rank implies
full complex rank.

\emph{3.1.} The polarized rank-one projector is a polynomial in $J^z$ and
belongs to the generated algebra. A cyclic vector together with its
rank-one projector forces a scalar commutant. Steps 2.1 and 3 establish
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fixed_point} in the certified sizes. The $J=0$ control has
rank $L+1$; no finite-rank calculation is an all-size induction.

\paragraph*{Size dependence.}
Because Eq.~\eqref{eq:fixed_point} is closed, $\ENinf$ costs one
eigendecomposition of $\Psym^{T_A}$ and no dynamics, which puts $L=14$ within
reach (Fig.~\ref{fig:ENinfL}). The values are $0.2231$, $0.4497$, $0.6465$,
$0.8238$, $0.9420$, $1.0109$ and $1.0336$ nats at $L=4,6,8,10,12,13,14$, with
$N_L=6,13,30,78,224,380,687$. These finite-size values do not establish an asymptotic coefficient; the bound
$\log2L$ and the exclusion of linear growth are what the data
support.

\paragraph*{Death-time convention.}
The algebraic condition is $\rho^{T_A}\succeq0$, whereas the experimental
threshold here is the last downward log-interpolated crossing of
$\EN=10^{-3}$. At integer periods one must also distinguish a finite observed
PPT tail from a proof of permanent entry. Threshold crossing occurs at
positive negativity and is not an exact zero.
The dephasing limiting coefficients quoted above come from the full slow
density. Checks on the entire crossing kernel/negative cluster, subsequent
intervals and final norm ball exclude returns for those limiting
trajectories~\cite{SM}. These are floating-point evaluations of analytic
bounds, not directed-rounding certificates. Neither three-size fits nor a
normalization identity proves $\gamma A\to\log2/2$.

\paragraph*{Provenance of the dephasing model.}
During the dissipative step, classical Gaussian phase increments satisfy
$\mathbb E[dW_jdW_k]=\gamma\Xi_{jk}\,dt$.
The Stratonovich-to-It\^o correction gives the dephasing part of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dissipator_site} exactly~\cite{SM}. This establishes the
accumulated noise channel; it does not replace
Eq.~\eqref{eq:selected_map} with a continuously noisy Hamiltonian period.
For independent unit Wiener increments $dB_c,dB_j$, the shared-line realization is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:onenoiseline}
dW_j=\sqrt{\gamma}\bigl(\sqrt\xi\,dB_c+\sqrt{1-\xi}\,dB_j\bigr),
\quad 0\le\xi\le1.
\end{equation}
For negative physical $\xi$, use a factorization of the positive-semidefinite
covariance. Increment correlations and white spectra have a finite
normalization; pointwise white-noise variances do not.
The $\gamma L^2$ decay of extreme magnetization coherences does not invalidate
this exact classical-noise average. Supported endpoint fixed states commute
with $J^z$ and are annihilated by the collective dephasing generator.

A classical Hermitian field cannot model spontaneous emission. In the
amplitude problem use $C=\sum_jc_j$ with $c=\ket0\!\bra1$ and separate
rates $\gamma(n_{\rm th}+1)$, $\gamma n_{\rm th}$. This channel is nonunital,
and its lifetime coefficient depends on dissipative
micromotion as well as the stationary carriers~\cite{SM}. Quantum-bath
weak coupling requires comparison of transition rates with microscopic
bath scales: for example, a maximal-spin $L=10$, $m=0$ collective transition
has rate $30\gamma$ at zero temperature, not merely $L\gamma$.
These physical assumptions cannot be inferred from a chosen phenomenological
Lindblad rate. The dephasing theorem and the conditional amplitude results
therefore remain separate.
