1. **Peripheral contraction and equality.** Assume M(X)=lambda X and |lambda|=1. Set Y=UF X UF^dagger. Since unitary conjugation preserves the Hilbert–Schmidt norm and E is its contraction, ||X||2=||M(X)||2=||E(Y)||2<=||Y||2=||X||2.
   1.1. In a total-Z eigenbasis, E multiplies matrix units with eigenvalues s,s' by exp[-gamma(s-s')^2/2]. Gamma>0 implies every unequal-s block is strictly contracted and every equal-s block is unchanged.
   1.2. The equality in1 therefore requires Y to have no unequal-s block. Hence E(Y)=Y, [Jz,Y]=0 and Y=lambda X. The last equality gives [Jz,X]=0.
   1.3. Conjugate [Jz,X]=0 by UF. Since UI commutes with Jz and Y=lambda X, [UK Jz UK^dagger,X]=0. The rotated operator is cos(2b)Jz-sin(2b)Jy. Together with [Jz,X]=0 and sin(2b)!=0 this gives [Jy,X]=0, then [Jx,X]=0 by the su(2) commutator.
   1.4. UK therefore commutes with X. Removing UK from Y=lambda X gives UI X UI^dagger=lambda X. The converse follows directly: an X commuting with all three collective spins and satisfying this UI relation is unchanged by E and has the claimed peripheral relation. At lambda1 this identifies the fixed algebra with the commutant of Jx,Jy,Jz,UI.
2. **Why XdaggerX is fixed.** The restricted M is unital, completely positive and trace preserving. The Schwarz inequality gives M(XdaggerX)-M(X)dagger M(X)>=0.
   2.1. Trace preservation and |lambda|1 make the trace of that positive difference zero. A finite-dimensional positive matrix with zero trace is zero, so M(XdaggerX)=XdaggerX.
   2.2. If the fixed algebra on S is scalar, XdaggerX=c I_S. For nonzero X, c>0, so X is invertible on S. This uses finite dimension and does not follow for an arbitrary linear map.
3. **Excluding nontrivial peripheral phases.** Because P commutes with all the generators, a supported X can be extended by zero to the full Hilbert space; the relations in1 hold for this extension.
   3.1. The highest Jz eigenspace is one dimensional, spanned by v=|0...0>. Commutation [Jz,X]=0 therefore implies Xv=c0v. Also UIv=uv with |u|1 because v is a computational eigenvector of UI. No maximal-spin representation theorem is needed for this step.
   3.2. Applying UI X UI^dagger=lambda X to v gives c0=lambda c0. For lambda!=1 this forces Xv=0. But v is in S, contradicting invertibility from2.2. Thus no nonzero peripheral X with lambda!=1 exists.
   3.3. M is a Hilbert–Schmidt contraction, so its powers are bounded; a nontrivial Jordan block at a unit-modulus eigenvalue would produce polynomial norm growth and is impossible. Its fixed algebra is one dimensional and unitality/trace preservation select I_S/dimS as the unique stationary density. All remaining eigenvalues have modulus<1 and their Jordan contributions decay. Thus every supported density converges ordinarily to P/TrP, without time averaging.
